# Lean Muscle Gain Diet Plan — 24M, 165cm, 63kg, Hostel (India)

**Profile:** Intermediate lifter, 5 training days/week (90 min sessions), ~10k steps/day, non-vegetarian, no allergies/medical conditions reported.

Check your label and adjust the whey/oats/bread rows if the numbers are meaningfully different.
- You have mess/hostel access to basics like rice, roti, dal, and vegetables in addition to your personal stash.

**Why a small surplus, not a deficit or strict recomp:** Your BMI (~23) suggests you're not carrying excess fat to "recomp away." True body recomposition (building muscle while losing fat) works best for beginners or people with higher body fat — as an intermediate lifter, a small, controlled surplus (a "mini bulk") builds lean mass more reliably while keeping fat gain minimal, *if* you monitor and adjust (see the 2-week rule below).

**On protein counting:** whey, eggs, chicken, soya chunks, and dal all count toward your total, but they're not equal — whey/eggs/chicken/fish are complete, fast-absorbing proteins; soya chunks are a good complete plant protein; dal, roasted chana, oats, bread, and peanuts contribute *some* usable protein but are incomplete/slower and shouldn't be relied on as your main sources. The plan below leans on eggs, chicken, whey, and soya chunks as the backbone, with the rest as supporting contributors.

## Meal Plan — Training Days

### 1. Breakfast (~630 kcal | 38g protein | 80g carbs | 18g fat)
Eat within 30–45 min of waking.

| Food | Quantity | Calories | Protein | Carbs | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yogabar high-protein oats (dry) | 60 g (~½ cup) | ~230 kcal | ~12 g | ~34 g | ~5 g *(estimate — check pack)* |
| Toned milk | 200 ml | ~120 kcal | ~6.5 g | ~10 g | ~6 g |
| Banana | 1.5 medium (~150 g) | ~135 kcal | ~1.5 g | ~34 g | ~0.5 g |
| Chia seeds | 1 tsp (5 g) | ~24 kcal | ~0.8 g | ~2 g | ~1.5 g |
| 1 whole egg + 3 egg whites | 1 + 3 | ~120 kcal | ~17 g | ~0.5 g | ~5 g |

**Why 1 whole egg + 3 whites, not all whole or all whites:** the yolk carries choline, vitamin D, and healthy fat that support hormones and satiety — skipping it entirely isn't necessary. But eating 4 whole eggs daily adds more saturated fat than you need, so combining whole egg(s) with whites keeps protein high without excess fat.

**Prep (air fryer / kettle / blender only):** Boil eggs using hot water from the kettle (whites: 8–9 min; take one out early if you want a slightly softer whole egg), or make a simple egg + egg-white scramble in a small oven-safe bowl in the air fryer (160°C, ~8 min, stirring once). Soak the oats in hot kettle water for 5 min, or blend oats + milk + chia + half the banana into a quick shake in your portable blender; eat the rest of the banana on the side.

**Substitutions:** No oats → 2 extra slices of bread. No milk → water + reduce whey elsewhere by half a scoop to compensate. No eggs → 60g paneer or 1 extra whey scoop.

### 2. Lunch (~785 kcal | 52g protein | 102g carbs | 16g fat)
Eat around 12–1 PM, ideally 3+ hours after breakfast.

| Food | Quantity | Calories | Protein | Carbs | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cooked rice | 200 g | ~260 kcal | ~5.5 g | ~57 g | ~0.6 g |
| Dal (toor/moong), cooked | 1.5 cups (~225 g) | ~225 kcal | ~13.5 g | ~30 g | ~4.5 g |
| Chicken breast, air-fried | 90 g | ~150 kcal | ~28 g | 0 g | ~3 g |
| Mixed vegetable sabzi/salad | 150 g (1 tsp oil) | ~90 kcal | ~2 g | ~10 g | ~5 g |
| Curd/dahi | 100 g | ~60 kcal | ~3.5 g | ~5 g | ~3 g |

**Prep:** Marinate chicken breast in curd, turmeric, chili powder, and salt for 15+ min (or overnight in the fridge), then air-fry at 180°C for 12–15 min, flipping halfway. Rice, dal, and sabzi can come from the mess if cooking isn't practical — just try to keep portions close to the table above.

**Substitutions:** No chicken → 50g dry soya chunks (rehydrate in hot kettle water 10 min, then air-fry 8–10 min with spices) or 2 boiled eggs + extra dal. No curd → 200ml buttermilk/chaas.

Eat 60–90 min before training — adjust this window to your actual training clock time.

## Rest Day Adjustments
No training = slightly lower needs, and the "pre-workout" timing no longer applies:
- Move the pre-workout meal to a flexible afternoon snack — same food, any time.
- Skip the 4 dates in that meal (or swap for one small fruit) — saves ~80 kcal.
- Drop 1 roti at dinner (3 instead of 4) — saves ~70 kcal.
- This lands you around **~2,400–2,450 kcal**, close to your rest-day target, protein unchanged (~165g).

## Supplement Guidance

**Whey protein (Nakpro Performance):** Use it as a convenient top-up, not a replacement for whole food — it's what makes hitting ~165g protein/day realistic on a hostel budget/schedule. 1 scoop mixed in water any time protein is short; timing (pre vs post-workout) matters far less than hitting your daily total.

**Creatine monohydrate:** 3–5 g/day, any time of day — consistency matters more than timing. **No loading phase needed**; loading (20g/day for a week) only saturates your muscles ~5–7 days faster, which isn't meaningfully different for your goals. Take it with a meal or your pre/post-workout shake for easy habit-building. Drink enough water.

**Fish oil capsules:** Check the label specifically for **combined EPA + DHA content** (not total fish oil mg — a 1000mg capsule might only have 300mg of actual EPA+DHA). Aim for roughly 1–2g combined EPA+DHA/day for general anti-inflammatory/joint support around training. Take with a meal that has some fat for better absorption.

**Sodium Glycinate capsules:** This isn't a standard sports-nutrition supplement with well-established dosing for muscle building, so I don't have reliable guidance to give you on it here — please check the specific product's label/instructions, or ask a pharmacist/doctor what it's meant for (sodium-containing supplements are worth clearing with a doctor if you have any blood pressure concerns). It isn't necessary for the goals in this plan, so it's fine to skip unless it's been recommended to you for another reason.

**Nothing else is needed.** This food-based plan covers your requirements — no need for additional pre-workouts, BCAAs, or mass gainers.

## Weekly Monitoring System
Track these every week, same conditions each time:
1. **Bodyweight** — same time each morning, after using the bathroom, before eating. Use the **weekly average**, not a single day (day-to-day swings of 0.5–1kg from water/food are normal).
2. **Waist measurement** — at navel level, same time (morning), once a week.
3. **Gym performance** — log your key lift weights × reps; look for a general upward trend over 2–4 weeks.
4. **Hunger** — rate 1–10 before meals. Persistent 8–10 hunger may mean you're under-eating.
5. **Recovery** — sleep quality, how long soreness lasts, energy going into sessions.

## 2-Week Adjustment Rule
After 2 full weeks on the plan, check your weekly-average weight trend and waist:
- **Weight up <0.2kg/week AND strength stalled** → add ~150–200 kcal (extra milk, fruit, or a roti).
- **Weight up >0.5kg/week** → likely gaining fat faster than muscle → cut ~150–200 kcal (trim rice/roti portion).
- **Waist increasing noticeably faster than the scale** → same signal as above, prioritize the cut.
- **Realistic target pace:** ~0.25–0.4 kg/week average gain is a good sign of mostly-lean gains at your training level.
